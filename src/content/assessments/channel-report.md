## The brief

> Choose one channel from weeks 2 through 9. Explain its mechanism, its
> founding disclosure, and what it costs a defender to close it — as if you
> were writing the lecture that introduced it.

This is the course's own format, turned around: every lecture so far has
paired a mechanism with a historical case and a defensive cost. This report
asks you to produce that same triple yourself, for a channel of your
choosing, in enough depth that a reader who missed the corresponding lecture
could follow the argument from your report alone. A report that only
describes the mechanism, without the disclosure or the cost of defending
against it, has done a third of the assignment.

## What you submit

A single written report, 1,500-2,500 words, citing at least one primary
source for the channel's founding disclosure (the papers referenced in this
course's own lectures are acceptable choices, but you are not restricted to
them). No operational exploitation instructions — describe what the
disclosure demonstrated and how, not a working recipe for reproducing it
yourself.
