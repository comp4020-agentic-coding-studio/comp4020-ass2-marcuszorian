## The brief

> Choose a device and an adversary. Say which of the course's channels apply
> to that pairing, rank them, and estimate how much at least one of them
> actually leaks.

The capstone is not a survey of every channel the semester covered — it is
week 12's design method, run once, by you, on a device you choose. A vague
adversary ("someone trying to attack the device") makes every channel appear
to apply and produces a report with no argument in it; a precise one (a
named proximity, a named resource budget) rules most channels out
immediately, which is the point. The strongest reviews will look like they
covered less ground than a weaker one, because they spent their words on the
channels that actually survive the adversary's constraints.

## What you submit

A written design review: the device, the adversary stated precisely, the
taxonomy-based reasoning for which channels apply, a ranked list of what to
defend first, and a mutual-information estimate (following week 10's method)
for at least one channel on that list. No working exploit against the
chosen device is required or wanted — the deliverable is the design
judgement, not a demonstration.
