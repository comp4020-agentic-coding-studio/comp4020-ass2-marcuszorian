A course is one argument, not twelve topics. Weeks 2 through 8 take one
physical channel each and argue it from mechanism to disclosure to what it
costs to close; week 9 steps back to the precondition the shared-hardware
channels depend on; the last three weeks turn the accumulated list into a
rubric, a defensive discipline and a design method.

Below is the argument in twelve lines — what each week changes, rather than
what it covers. If a line could be removed without the rest weakening, that
week is the wrong week.

## What the course reads

Each week's readings are listed on its lecture page. Collected here in the
order the field produced them, they make a second argument: a 1973 memo that
named the problem before any of these channels had been measured, two
decades in which each disclosure was its own isolated result, and then a
turn — from roughly 2005 — toward papers whose contribution is a bound or a
countermeasure rather than another channel.
