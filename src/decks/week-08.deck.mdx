# Residue

A four-digit code was entered on this keypad and the person who entered it
has gone. How long do you have? And how long do you have for the harder
question, which is what order they pressed them in?

---

## What the finger left

Contact warms the key by roughly three degrees above ambient. The key cools
back through its own mass and the air, on a time constant set by the
material and by nothing the device is running.

Every other channel in this course stops when the computation stops. This
one starts then.

```notes
Mean intensity over each key's area, converted to kelvin above ambient.
Peak rise 3.0 K, exponential decay, fitted time constant 24 s for an ABS
keypad. The four curves are offset only by when each key was pressed: the
entry took four seconds, so the first key has a four-second head start on
the last.

Segmentation floor 0.25 K — below that a pressed key is not reliably
separable from an unpressed one. The last curve crosses it at 56 seconds.
```

---

## The first window is the obvious one

Every curve falls through the segmentation floor at about the same moment,
because they differ by four seconds out of ninety.

Fifty-six seconds to recover *which* four keys were pressed. That is the
number a defender who has read the abstract will quote.

# Fifty-six and twenty-eight

The set of keys survives twice as long as their order.

A defender working from one number is working from the wrong one, and it is
the generous one.

---

## Nobody was there to defend

Shield the room before the monitor is on. Rewrite the comparison before it
runs. Disable deduplication before the tenant arrives.

There is no equivalent here. The leak begins after the interaction ends and
after an attacker's presence would have been noticed. No detection, no
interruption, no incident.

---

## One lever, and it is not software

The time constant is a material property. Change the keypad, or change
nothing.

| Keypad | Fitted constant | Set recoverable | Order recoverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| ABS plastic | 24 s | 56 s | 28 s |
| Brushed aluminium | 4 s | 9 s | 5 s |

Aluminium does not close the channel. It moves the shorter window inside the
time it takes to walk to the keypad, which is the same thing to a defender
and a different thing to an argument.

---

## Before the Bench

Bring something that reads per-pixel intensity out of an image and plots a
series. The Bench supplies ninety seconds of frames after a known four-digit
entry.

Fit the decay, then plot the pairwise differences beside the raw series and
find both windows. Keep the constant: week 10 puts this channel on a common
rubric, and your fitted number is what it wants.
