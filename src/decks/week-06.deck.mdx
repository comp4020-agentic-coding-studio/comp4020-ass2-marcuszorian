# Flush, wait, reload

You can time your own memory accesses. Nothing privileged about that. What
does it tell you about what somebody else's process just did?

---

## Step 1 — flush

Evict one named line of memory from the cache. Not the victim's copy: the
shared copy, the one both processes are reading because the operating system
mapped the same physical page into both.

Afterwards, nobody's cache holds it.

---

## Step 2 — wait

Let the victim run for a fixed interval, then stop waiting.

The interval is the resolution. Too long and several accesses collapse into
one observation; too short and the measurement costs more than it reads.

---

## Step 3 — reload, and time it

Read that same line and time the read.

Fast means something pulled it back into the cache while you waited. Slow
means nothing did. One bit, per line, per interval.

## Two preconditions, and they are the point

- **Shared pages.** The attacker has to be able to *name* the line it
  flushes — a shared library, or a hypervisor deduplicating identical pages.
  This is not a channel between two processes that share nothing.
- **A last-level cache shared across the package.** `clflush` reaches it, so
  attacker and victim need not be on the same core.

---

## When the pages are not shared

| Property | FLUSH+RELOAD | PRIME+PROBE |
|---|---|---|
| Needs shared memory | yes | no |
| What it observes | a named line | a cache set |
| Resolution | one line | one set, aliased |
| Noise | low | higher |

Turning off page deduplication is the defence most providers chose. It costs
the attacker resolution, not access.

---

## Where this course stops

Transient-execution attacks use this same cache as their read-out channel.
Their *source* is speculative work that never architecturally happened.

That is a different argument on different prerequisites, and this course
does not teach it.

---

## Before the Bench

Two numbers for your own machine:

- **cache line size** — `getconf LEVEL1_DCACHE_LINESIZE`
- **hit and miss latencies** — your CPU vendor's optimisation manual

The Bench classifies your own timings against that threshold, not this
deck's.

# No privilege at any step

Not a debugger, not administrator rights, not a bug in the other process.

That absence is why this channel is hard to close by policy.
