# The leakage taxonomy

Seven channels, seven disclosures, seven separate arguments. This week adds
no eighth. Which of them would you close first, and what measurement would
defend the choice?

---

## The six axes

- **medium** — which physical quantity carries it out
- **proximity** — how close, and for how long
- **availability** — during the computation, or only after
- **rate** — bits of the secret per measurement
- **volume** — measurements needed before the estimate settles
- **cost to close** — what the defender gives up

Four you can fill in from your own Bench notes. Two you cannot.

## "It leaks" is not a number

Every channel so far was shown to leak *something*. None of those
demonstrations said *how much*, in bits, under realistic noise.

Rate and volume are that gap.

---

## Mutual information, briefly

For a secret `S` and a measurement `M` of it:

```
I(S; M) = H(S) - H(S | M)
```

How much uncertainty about `S` remains, on average, once you know `M`. Zero
means the channel told you nothing. `H(S)` means it told you everything.

On too few samples it reports a leak that is not there. Recompute it as the
sample count grows and watch where it stops moving.

## Placing the channels

| Channel | Proximity | Availability | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acoustic (wk 2) | same room | during | low, many needed |
| Power (wk 3) | physical contact | during | high, few traces |
| Timing (wk 4) | network path | during | very low, very many |
| Cache (wk 6) | same package, any core, shared pages | during | medium |
| Thermal (wk 8) | none during, camera after | after only | low, decays |

Electromagnetic (wk 5) and optical (wk 7) are yours to place. There is no
single correct table — the Bench and the capstone both test the reasoning,
not the entries.

---

## Two orderings, not one

Rank the seven by **cost to the attacker**. Now rank them again by **cost to
the defender**.

The lists disagree.

- cheap to exploit, cheap to close → a bug, already fixed
- costly to exploit, costly to close → someone else's threat model
- **cheap to exploit, costly to close** → the ones that matter

# A number a defender can act on

"0.2 bits per trace, needs physical contact" and "6 bits per trace, over the
network" are not the same engineering problem, even though both are,
technically, side channels.

Bring your seven-row table and both orderings to the Bench.
