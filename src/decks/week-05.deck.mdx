# The last 20 dB

Shielding a room is a purchase, not a decision. What does each additional
10 dB cost, and where on that curve does everyone who is not a government
stop buying?

---

## Why the emission is reconstructible

A display drives its image on a fixed schedule and repeats the frame sixty
times a second. A receiver that knows the schedule can add the repeats
together.

That is the whole reason this is a picture rather than a hiss: the signal is
periodic and the noise is not.

---

## What the target actually is

Attenuation is not measured against audibility. It is measured against how
long an attacker is willing to sit outside.

Coherent averaging of N repeats buys back 10·log₁₀(N) dB. Sixty frames a
second is the exchange rate.

---

## The exchange rate, written out

| Attenuation | Repeats needed to undo it | Time on a 60 Hz display |
|---|---|---|
| 10 dB | 10 | 0.17 s |
| 20 dB | 100 | 1.7 s |
| 40 dB | 10,000 | 2.8 min |
| 60 dB | 1,000,000 | 4.6 hours |
| 80 dB | 100,000,000 | 19 days |

The first two rows are free to the attacker. The defender pays for all five.

```notes
This is the number that makes the cost curve legible. A defender thinking in
decibels is thinking in a unit the attacker converts to patience, and the
conversion is brutally cheap until roughly 40 dB.

19 days also assumes the target displays the same frame for 19 days, which
is where the arithmetic stops being the binding constraint and the threat
model takes over.
```

---

## What each band costs to build

| Target | What buys it | Where the leak has moved |
|---|---|---|
| 20 dB | conductive paint or foil on the walls | the door |
| 40 dB | mesh through every wall, floor and ceiling | ventilation and cable entries |
| 60 dB | waveguide vents, filtered mains, RF door gasket | the gasket's maintenance schedule |
| 80 dB | welded enclosure, isolated power, no penetrations | the people who have to work inside it |

Each row closes the dominant leak and promotes the next one.

# The curve decides who pays

A build to 80 dB costs five times a build to 60 dB. The last 20 dB is
four-fifths of the bill.

TEMPEST is still a government practice, not because the mechanism stayed
secret — van Eck published in 1985 — but because nobody else was ever going
to buy the right-hand end of the curve.

---

## Before the Bench

Bring a stated attenuation target and the willingness to cost a room instead
of capture one: mesh, seams, penetrations, door.

The Bench asks for a bill of materials and one sentence separating what the
room costs from what the device costs. Compare curves with another pair
afterwards — your totals will disagree and your shapes should not.
